<h2>One micronutrient, one day (ELI5)</h2>
<p>Imagine your body as a bustling city. Calcium is the material that holds up the scaffolding, but it is also one of the tiny electrical sparks that let nerves talk and muscles move. The same mineral can be a beam and a message. That double duty is why it shows up in almost every story about bones, cells and signalling — and why a note about it has to stay humble about what a single nutrient can do.</p>

<h2>What the sources actually say</h2>
<p>The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheet, checked 28 Nov 2026, treats calcium as both a structural mineral and a signalling ion. Its health-professional page surveys bone health in older adults, cancer, cardiovascular disease, preeclampsia, weight management and metabolic syndrome — as areas of research, not as promises.</p>
<p>EFSA's 2015 scientific opinion took a different route: it derived dietary reference values from balance studies rather than from a single outcome. Two authorities, two methods. We are listing what they examined, not turning their numbers into instructions for anyone.</p>

<h2>What we will not say</h2>
<p>No "take X milligrams", no target value presented as a personal instruction, no promise about bones, hearts, cancers or ageing. Reference values belong to the sources that publish them. This is an evidence note, not a recommendation — and not a place to buy anything.</p>
