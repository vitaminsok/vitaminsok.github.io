<h2>One micronutrient, one day</h2>
<p>Vitamin D is not one molecule but a small family of fat-soluble compounds. It is an organic molecule — a vitamin in the strict sense, unlike calcium or magnesium.</p>

<h2>What the sources actually say</h2>
<p>NIH ODS (updated 27 June 2025) says vitamin D sufficiency prevents rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults, and that together with calcium it helps protect older adults from osteoporosis. The same fact sheet is careful: optimal serum 25(OH)D concentrations for bone and general health have not been established, because they likely vary by life stage, race and ethnicity, and by the measure used.</p>
<p>NIH also notes that the committee setting DRIs found the evidence inadequate or too contradictory to conclude effects beyond bone health. EFSA's 2016 opinion says Average Requirements and Population Reference Intakes cannot be derived and therefore defines Adequate Intakes — a source decision, not a target we set for readers.</p>

<h2>What we will not say</h2>
<p>No “take X micrograms”, no blood-value target as a personal instruction, no promise about cardiovascular risk, cancer, depression or fractures. Any number belongs to the cited source, with its date and link.</p>
